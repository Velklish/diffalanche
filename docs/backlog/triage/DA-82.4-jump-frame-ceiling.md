# DA-82.4 · The jump and composer lines could hold a frame count against a ceiling

- **Scope:** 11-perf (see [reference](../../reference/README.md))
- **Created:** 2026-09-29
- **Dependencies:** none
- **Parent:** DA-82.3
- **Cost:** major

## Context

Finding discovered while working on DA-82.3. The unpaced harness charges a frame
with something to draw what drawing it costs, a millisecond or two, where the
reader's 60 Hz display charges up to 16.7 ms; an idle frame still costs it about
17.6 ms. So an added frame that only moves work the page does anyway earlier is
outside the gate's `ms` lines, and an added idle frame is not; DA-82.3 wrote that
into
[11-perf.md](../../reference/11-perf.md#what-a-frame-costs-unpaced-and-what-the-gate-does-not-see)
with its measurements, as standing until this card is decided.

Evidence, the 4-core cloud container of 2026-09-29, Chromium 141 headless shell,
one-minute load 0.7–2.3 on 4 cores: a `requestAnimationFrame` counter installed
from the harness side (`page.evaluate`, no change to `src/ui`) around each call
counted the frames the call spans, in 25 `perf`-shaped processes of three jumps
each:

- the jump, `perf.jumpToFile`, unpaced: 3 frames in 15 of 15 jumps on the page as
  it ships (5 processes); 4 in 27 of 27 with an `await afterPaint()` added before
  the first scroll of `revealCard` (10 processes, of which one failed before
  printing, so 9 × 3);
- the jump at 60 Hz: 3 in 15 of 15 and 4 in 15 of 15 (5 processes each);
- the composer, `perf.openComposer`, unpaced: 1 frame in 14 of 14, one opening in
  each of the 14 unpaced processes that printed;
- the session switch: 6 to 62 frames, since the counter keeps frames coming while
  the page waits for the server — it counts the wait, not the work.

So a frame count is cheap and was deterministic for the jump and the composer, and
is not a measure at all for the switch, the update or the first render. A ceiling
on it is a new row of the budget table in `perf/budgets.ts`, and whether the gate
gets one is not a worker's call.
