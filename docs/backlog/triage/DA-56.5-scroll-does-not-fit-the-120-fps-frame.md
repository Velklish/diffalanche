# DA-56.5 · The scroll does not fit inside the frame of 120 fps on an M1 Pro, and nobody has looked for why

- **Scope:** 08-ui, 11-perf (see [reference](../../reference/README.md))
- **Created:** 2026-09-21
- **Cost:** major
- **Dependencies:** none

## Context

Filed so that the number in `perf/budgets.ts` is not read as the target. On
2026-09-21 the owner set the `Scrolling the diff: CPU per frame` gate to 9.5 ms
because that is what this machine reaches ([DA-56.4](DA-56.4-cpu-per-frame-misses-its-budget-at-rest.md),
[ADR-013](../../adr/adr-013-perf-gate-off-ci.md)). The goal did not change:
`docs/SPEC.md` section 6 asks for 120 fps, whose frame is **8.3 ms**, and the
gap between the two is this entry.

What the measurement says, and all it says:

- Nine commits of the main branch between `1193ab3` and `ed81928`, one
  `bun run perf` each, load average 7.6 to 10.8: **8.5, 8.8, 9.1, 8.9, 8.9, 8.8,
  8.7, 8.8, 8.7 ms.** No trend, and no commit of that range responsible.
- Six more runs over three trees in a quieter window: 8.6 to 9.0 ms.
- The lowest single repetition of any of those runs was 8.5 ms.

So the scroll has been over the frame for the whole of the range anybody has
measured, which is where the range ends and not where the cause begins.
Everything before `1193ab3` is unmeasured.

The measurement is the 600-frame stress scroll of the harness, which is far
faster than a person scrolls — that is deliberate
([11-perf.md](../../reference/11-perf.md)). Whether the same CPU cost appears at
a human scroll rate is not known either: nobody has measured it.

## Work to do

- Find where the CPU per frame goes. A Chromium trace over the harness's scroll,
  with the categories the renderer attributes work to, is the first step and
  costs one run; `perf/harness.ts` already drives the scroll through
  `window.__perf.scrollRun`, so a trace can be attached to the same page rather
  than to a hand-driven one.
- Measure at a human scroll rate beside the stress rate. If the gap only exists
  at 600 frames of forced scrolling, the goal and the gate mean different things
  and the specification should say which one 120 fps is about.
- Look further back than `1193ab3` before concluding it was ever inside the
  frame. The one older reading anybody has is DA-54's 8.2 ms from 2026-09-10,
  and re-measuring DA-54's own commit today gave 8.8 — so that reading is not
  evidence of a better past, it is evidence of a different machine state.

## Out of scope

- The gate's number, which is 9.5 and was decided with its evidence in
  [DA-56.4](DA-56.4-cpu-per-frame-misses-its-budget-at-rest.md). Lowering it is
  the *result* of this work, not part of it.
- The load precondition (ADR-013), which is landed and under whose ceiling every
  reading above was taken.

## Verification

- A named cause, with the share of the frame it accounts for, measured rather
  than reasoned about.
- If the cause is fixed: `bun run perf` on a quiet machine measures under 8.3 ms
  three times, and the gate's number goes back to 8.3 in the same pass.
- If it is not worth fixing: the specification says so, with the number this
  machine delivers written into section 6 instead of the frame of 120 fps.
