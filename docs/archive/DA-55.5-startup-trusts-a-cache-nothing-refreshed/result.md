# DA-55.5 · Result

**Closed 2026-09-23.** Completed, with the card's first candidate: the startup reads the current session's scope from the working tree before the socket opens, so no cache is trusted at the moment nothing has refreshed any. `Watcher.refresh()` (`src/core/watcher/index.ts`) queues one full read of the followed session's scope in the watcher's own rescan queue, hands the result over through `onRescan` and writes `diff.json` under the session lock; `src/server/serve.ts` awaits it right after `startWatcher` and before the warm-up. It lives in the watcher's queue rather than in the review service because a service-side "distrust the first build" races with a rescan that starts meanwhile — the rescan patches the stale file, and the adopted cache then lays stale-plus-one-repository over the fresh build; in the queue the rescan waits for the refresh and patches what it wrote. The rescan's fall-through to a full read became `rescanSession`, which both use. No current session, or a `review.json` that cannot be read, and `refresh` does nothing: the warm-up reports as before. The price, measured on a busy machine and recorded in `07-server.md`: `serve` to a listening socket took a median of 552 and 489 ms over two sets of five starts, against 39 ms for the build that trusted the cache, on the synthetic review's 21 repositories at a load average of 21–23 on 8 cores — an upper bound, and it grows with the number of repositories in the scope. The two cheaper candidates were weighed and left, with reasons in `07-server.md`: trusting the cache until the first rescan is wrong indefinitely for a repository nobody touches, and comparing the recorded base `sha` misses the uncommitted edit, the common case.
