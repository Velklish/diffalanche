# DA-89 · Result

**Closed 2026-09-18.** Completed. `DEFAULT_TIMEOUT_MS` is no longer an independent number: it equals `DEFAULT_STALE_MS`, and `withLock` raises an explicit `timeoutMs` that sits below `staleMs` up to it, so the invariant "the wait is never shorter than the lease" holds on the defaults and on any pair passed in, and cannot be reopened by editing one constant. A lock left behind by a killed writer is therefore taken over rather than outliving the wait: before this, a writer arriving in the first twenty seconds after the holder died waited its ten seconds out and refused, naming a process that was not there. The refusal now names what the waiter actually read out of the lock — the holder's pid, when it was taken, and its `expiresAt` — and, after review, only when the claim is whole: with no pid, no acquisition time or no `expiresAt` it says "held by a writer that has not claimed it" instead of assembling a holder out of `undefined`. The first of the task's three candidates was taken: shortening the lease takes the margin away from the full scan that runs under the lock (`src/core/change-set.ts:226-256`), and stretching the deadline to the `expiresAt` that was read buys the same result with arithmetic that then needs a caveat against a hand-edited value. The price is that against a **live** long holder a writer now waits up to 30 s instead of 10 s before being refused; against a **dead** one it gets the lock, and that is the only case that occurs at all when a writer has been killed. The signal handler and takeover by pid liveness stay outside the task by its own Out of scope: the lock is still required to outlive a holder that vanished without warning, and it does.
