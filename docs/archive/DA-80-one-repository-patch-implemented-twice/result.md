**Closed 2026-09-23.** Completed. Patching one repository into `diff.json` is one pure function, `replaceRepository(cached, change)` in `src/core/change-set.ts`, called by the CLI's `refreshRepository` and by the watcher's `rescanRepository`; the lock, the read, the base-and-scope check and the write stay with each caller, because what differs between them — the watcher's `sameChange` short-circuit and its `Rescan` outcome, the CLI's fall-through to a full scan — would otherwise need a callback per difference (`02-git.md` › "Patching one repository"). The contract was the owner's choice, recorded in the task card on 2026-09-23: the scan-level warnings travel in `DiffCache` in a form that survives a patch. That form is `DiffCache.rootWarnings`, the share of `warnings` produced by the walk of the root (`worktree of …`, `directory cannot be read`, `root is itself a repository`) and by the scope (`in the scope of this review task, but not a repository under the root`); `warnings` stays the full list, so `diff --json`, `/api/warnings` and the UI see the shape they saw plus one field, and `diff --repo` narrows both lists. The marker of a cache written before the field is the field's absence, not `SCHEMA_VERSION`: that version is also written into `review.json` and `comments.json`, and raising it would make every earlier build refuse those files. After review #3 such a cache is **read as it is** by every reader and refused only by the two patching writers, through `patchable(cache, base, scope)`, which then fall back to a full scan that writes the field — so a closed task keeps its counters in the sessions list. Every list of the cache is sorted at one point, `cache()`, which both a full scan and a patch build through; a full scan therefore also writes its warnings sorted now, where before it wrote the walk's, then the scope's, then each repository's in that order.
