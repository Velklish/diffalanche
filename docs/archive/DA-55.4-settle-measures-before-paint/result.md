# DA-55.4 · Result

**Closed 2026-09-19.** Completed. The hypothesis holds, with one correction to it. `settle()` waited one frame and then measured; the correction to the card's own wording is that it is not the **paint** that matters — `getBoundingClientRect` forces layout, so an unpainted frame still measures correctly — it is the **commit**. A store write only schedules React's work, and under load React can land it after that single frame; the measurement then reads a DOM without the patch, finds a delta of nothing, corrects nothing, and the growth arrives when nobody is looking. That is the `44` of two diff rows, and it is why it only appears under load. `settle()` now watches the anchor over up to four frames: a move is corrected and ends the wait; a frame where nothing moved ends it only once the page's own height has changed, which is what tells a patch that landed above nothing from one that has not landed yet. A patch that changes neither the height nor the anchor spends the whole budget and scrolls nothing. A reader who scrolls while this is waiting ends the wait untouched — `capture()` records `window.scrollY` with the anchor, and a page moved by something other than the correction is the reader's own doing.

**Verification — recorded, not argued.** `window.__perf.settles` reports per correction the delta, the page height before, **at the first frame**, **at the frame the loop stopped on**, and after, and `grewAfter`: the growth that arrived after the loop finished, counted from the stopping frame, so it is non-zero only when `settle()` never saw the patch at all. `heightAtMeasure` is the first frame and stays for diagnosis: `heightAtMeasure !== heightAtEnd` is what says the commit was late. That definition is itself a correction the isolated review made — `grewAfter` first shipped counted from the **first** frame, which made it non-zero exactly when the loop had done its job, so the assertion in the spec asserted "the commit was not late" rather than the property. Rather than waiting for the rare red, the condition was forced in a browser by delaying the store write 24 ms past the frame, with the reading probe placed as `e2e/live.spec.ts` places it; thirty edits per run, taken **after** the count was corrected: the loop undisturbed — `heightAtMeasure == heightAtEnd`, `delta 66, corrected true, grewAfter 0`, position held 30/30; the loop with the commit delayed — `heightAtMeasure 74481 < heightAtEnd 74547`, `delta 66, corrected true, grewAfter 0`, held 30/30; the one-frame wait with the commit delayed — `heightAtMeasure == heightBefore`, `delta 0`, `corrected false`, **moved 89.56 → 155.56 on 30 of 30**. Three runs and not four: the one-frame wait *undisturbed* was not re-run after the count changed, so it has no row rather than a borrowed one. Four earlier runs of sixty edits each, taken under the old count, agree about the reading position, and their `grewAfter` figures are not quoted because they were taken under the count this task went on to change. **Which half of the record has teeth**, from the same runs: under the forced late commit the one-frame wait moved the position on 30 of 30 and reported `corrected: false` on 30 of 30, while `grewAfter` was non-zero on only 17 — `corrected` is the reliable half and the spec asserts both. `bun run perf` was green on every line at `28e0c7f`, where this landed — `update` at 289 of 300 — so the extra frames did not cost the budget, which was the risk worth naming; `bun run test:ui` 95 passed. **Integration into `main`:** `28e0c7f` cherry-picked onto the DA-93 commit with no conflict, plus the DA-55.4 half of the two-prefix commit `5fbd6d8` — `CHANGELOG.md`, `docs/reference/08-ui.md`, `e2e/live.spec.ts`, `src/ui/live.ts`, `src/ui/perf.ts`, all applied cleanly.

**Not covered, and this matters more than the rest.**

- **The fix has no deterministic regression test.** `e2e/live.spec.ts` reddens only on the rare coincidence that produced the original report. The assertion added there — `corrected: true`, `grewAfter: 0` on the record — fixes the contract in the suite, but it passes on the old code undisturbed. The deterministic evidence is the forced experiment above, and it is an experiment rather than a test: nothing in the suite reproduces it.
- **`flushSync` around the store write is an unmeasured alternative, not a rejected one.** It was tried first; its run failed and the worker took that for the cause. It was not: a `git checkout` of `live.ts` a step earlier had removed the instrumentation, so no record was ever written and the recorder timed out waiting for one. The diagnosis was wrong and `flushSync` was never fairly measured. The loop was chosen afterwards on its own merits — it keeps `live.ts` free of `react-dom`, and it is literally what the card names as the thing to change — but if its frame budget ever becomes a problem, `flushSync` is the first alternative to try, and it starts from no evidence against it.
- `SETTLE_FRAMES = 4` is a number chosen to cover a 24 ms delay at 60 Hz with room, not measured against the real distribution of late commits, which nobody has.
- **The reader-scroll guard has no test.** Exercising it means landing a reader's scroll inside a window of at most four frames between the patch and the correction, and the worker did not reproduce it deterministically. A check that does not demonstrably reach what it names is worse than none, and this pass produced two of those before the probe caught them.
