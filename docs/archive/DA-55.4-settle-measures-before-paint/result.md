**Closed 2026-09-19.** Completed. The hypothesis holds, with one correction to it. `settle()` waited one frame and then measured; the correction to the card's own wording is that it is not the **paint** that matters — `getBoundingClientRect` forces layout, so an unpainted frame still measures correctly — it is the **commit**. A store write only schedules React's work, and under load React can land it after that single frame; the measurement then reads a DOM without the patch, finds a delta of nothing, corrects nothing, and the growth arrives when nobody is looking. That is the `44` of two diff rows, and it is why it only appears under load. `settle()` now watches the anchor over up to four frames: a move is corrected and ends the wait; a frame where nothing moved ends it only once the page's own height has changed, which is what tells a patch that landed above nothing from one that has not landed yet. A patch that changes neither the height nor the anchor spends the whole budget and scrolls nothing. A reader who scrolls while this is waiting ends the wait untouched — `capture()` records `window.scrollY` with the anchor, and a page moved by something other than the correction is the reader's own doing.
